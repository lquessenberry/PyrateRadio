# Gulf Coast & Caribbean Monitoring

Public frequencies used for regional maritime and inter-island traffic. Listening to open transmissions is legal. This is for situational awareness, not targeting.

## Marine MF/HF (USB)

### 2 MHz Band
- 2182 kHz — international distress/calling
- 2203 kHz — Gulf of Mexico
- 2214, 2638, 2670, 2738, 2830 kHz (various regional uses)

### 4 MHz Band (~4000–4438 kHz)
- Simplex: 4146 / 4149 and nearby
- Distress voice: 4125 kHz
- Caribbean weather and sailing nets common here

### 6 MHz & 8 MHz
- 6215 kHz — distress voice
- 8 MHz heavily used in Caribbean (8104, 8137, 8152, 8188 and nearby for weather, safety, cruiser nets)

Higher marine HF (12/16 MHz) opens for longer paths but is less consistent for Gulf-to-Caribbean distances.

## Amateur Bands with Regional Activity

### 80 m (3.5–4.0 MHz, LSB)
- Caribbean and Gulf weather/emergency nets (examples around 3815, 3930, etc.)
- Nighttime regional coverage

### 40 m (7 MHz, LSB)
- Dense Caribbean island nets (7.133, 7.145–7.176, 7.200, 7.268, and nearby)
- Good daytime and evening regional reach

## Propagation Notes

Lower end (2–4 MHz and 80 m) favors night. 6–8 MHz and 40 m favor day. Monitor the standard marine USB allocations plus 80 m and 40 m for the highest density of regional analog traffic.
