# Key Bands for Analog Failover

In a genuine analog failover (digital networks, cellular, and most linked systems dark), traffic concentrates where people already have radios and the physics supports useful range.

## Highest Activity

### 11 m CB (27 MHz)
- Legal, no license, 4 W AM / 12 W SSB
- Large installed base (trucks, rural, older vehicles)
- Local ground-wave plus occasional skip
- Channels 19, 9, and upper SSB (35–40) most active

### 40 m (7 MHz) & 80 m (3.5–4 MHz)
- Workhorse regional bands for prepared operators
- Nighttime and daytime regional coverage
- Dense amateur activity and emergency nets

### 20 m (14 MHz)
- Longer-range daytime contacts
- More consistent than upper HF in declining solar conditions

## Intermittent but Useful

### 10 m / 12 m / 15 m
- Excellent when open (solar max or sporadic-E)
- Quiet noise floor
- Similar propagation behavior to 11 m
- Openings are condition-dependent

## Local Only

### VHF/UHF (2 m, 70 cm, GMRS, FRS)
- Neighborhood and short-range simplex
- High density of handhelds
- Coverage collapses to line-of-sight without infrastructure

## Practical Takeaway

CB for the biggest uncoordinated crowd and immediate local utility. 40 m and 80 m for prepared HF operators and reliable regional reach. Upper HF for opportunistic long-range when the ionosphere cooperates.

Local analog simplex remains usable on all of these even when skip is absent.
